Compliance

Keep it in good standing

An entity stays active by filing its reports on time. Each state sets the due dates, and the state can change an entity's status when a report is missed.

What a due date is

A due date is the last day a filing can be made without a penalty or a change in status. Annual reports are the most common. Some states also ask for an update when the agent or an officer changes.

Where to find yours

The state where the entity was formed publishes its filing calendar and its entity record. Check the record for the next due date, and check that the agent named on it is correct. Charter House puts this date on the compliance calendar when the entity is set up.

Late is a status change
A missed due date can move an entity to a status the state marks as not in good standing. Put the date on the calendar early, not on the day it is due.

What we keep

The calendar
One list of every recurring due date for each entity, with the state and the date.
The record
Each filed report, each state notice and each approval, kept with the entity.
