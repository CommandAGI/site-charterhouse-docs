Guide

An entity has three records. Keep all three.

A business entity lives in three places: the state register, the federal tax record and the calendar of filings that keep it active. This guide explains each one in plain terms. It is written by Charter House, which forms and maintains entities through CommandAGI.

The three records

State register
The state where the entity was formed holds its formation papers and its list of filings. The state decides whether the entity is active.
Federal tax record
The IRS gives the entity an employer identification number (EIN). The EIN is used for banking, payroll and tax returns.
Calendar
Each state sets its own due dates for annual reports and other recurring filings. A missed date can cost the entity its active status.
Charter House rule
Every filing is recorded in the entity record before it is sent. We write the record first, so the record always shows what was sent.

Where to start

If the entity does not exist yet, read Form an entity. If it exists, read Keep it in good standing and check its due dates today.

What this guide is not

This guide describes how the work is done. It is not legal or tax advice. Rules differ by state and by entity type, so each job is checked against the rules of the state where the entity is registered before anything is filed.
